# Aisthesis

*Readability in a textual workspace.*

The principles of visual communication in a working space. Genesis designs the workspace. Praxis operates it. Aisthesis makes it readable — and shows what to do next.

Every visual choice carries information or noise. Aisthesis provides the principles to tell the difference.

---

## The idea

A Markdown file looks like plain text. It is not. It carries information on several simultaneous layers. A reader — human or AI — does not just read words. They perceive structure, visual weight, signals, symbols, spatial forms.

Readability is not a property of the text. It is a property of the orchestration of its layers.

---

## Meaning and form

Everything Aisthesis says rests on one distinction.

**Meaning** is what the author wants to stand out: this matters more than the rest, this belongs to that, this is in such a state. It is decided while writing, and it depends on no tool.

**Form** is how that meaning is made visible: structure, position, emphasis, typeface, colour, language, format. Form changes with the medium — a text file, a web page, a status screen — and can change without touching the meaning.

Aisthesis lives on the side of meaning. Its principles say what must reach the eye; the choice of means comes afterwards, for the reader's eye and the medium at hand.

**Emphasis exists only if it is perceived.** The author knows what they meant to mark; the reader receives only what they see. A means the eye cannot separate, or that the rendering erases, leaves the intention without effect — and the failure is silent: the author believes they signalled, the reader does not know they missed anything. Distinction belongs to the one who perceives, not to the intention of the one who writes.

Readability is thought at the level of meaning and checked in the rendering.

---

## What the reader does

Aisthesis is organised around what the reader *does* — not around the medium. Five acts, each developed in [reader](references/reader.md).

1. **Perceive before reading.** The eye precedes the brain: density, structure and signals decide whether a page is read, scanned or closed, before the first word. Structure is attention architecture; space sets the cadence; a sign in a name tells the *nature* of a place, a numeric prefix its *position*.
2. **Read at two speeds.** One text serves the one who scans and the one who reads deeply. **Bold** is the scan channel, *italic* the deep-read channel, `code` the literal. A callout is a contract — go fast, but don't miss this. A heading must suffice to decide whether to read on.
3. **Write to be read.** One idea per unit. Prose when thought unfolds: a list asserts its items are parallel, and lies when they are not. Peer-to-peer tone — propose, don't prescribe. Prose is the richest register and the riskiest: its defects do not look like defects.
4. **Understand without reading.** Information has a shape — ask *what shape does this have?*, not *how do I write it?* Tables for what compares, lists and trees for what nests, diagrams for flows and states, images when the referent is visual. When a subject has more dimensions than prose can carry at once, it demands a visual artefact so that one can think about it at all.
5. **Show before concluding.** Start with what is, let the reader see, then let them conclude: the current state, the distinction that sheds light, how it works, a real case, what it yields, open questions as invitations.

## What governs the whole

Four principles apply to every means at once. Each regulates a different dimension.

| Principle | Regulates | What fails without it |
|---|---|---|
| Just measure | quantity | excess — each signal drowns the others |
| Rhythm | sequence | uniformity — all dense exhausts, all airy scatters |
| A code is learned | constancy | the unstable sign — the reader falls back to reading |
| Signal without imposing | territory | the invasive signal — it takes over the reading |

- **Just measure.** Every means first serves, then harms: emphasis distinguishes while it is rare. The central anti-pattern is excess, not bareness — each addition must still distinguish something, or it subtracts.
- **Rhythm.** The reader moves by alternation — dense then light, detail then overview. A separator says the register changes; length itself speaks.
- **A code is learned.** A sign speaks before reading only when its code is **closed** — small enough to retain, not growing with each use — and **constant** — one sign, one meaning, wherever it appears.
- **Signal without imposing.** A signal enters the reader's space as a guest: proportionate to what it has to say, where the reader knows to find it, and it stays there.

Developed, with the sources that confirm them, in [whole](references/whole.md).

## The bridge to action

**Affordance.** A document signals the interaction it expects — read, contribute, execute, navigate — before its first line: by its name (declarative invites reading, imperative invites action), its case, its position in the structure, its ending. The same signal must serve a human, through habit, and an agent, through instruction. Developed in [whole](references/whole.md).

## Reach

Aisthesis was born in the textual workspace — Markdown files in a folder — and that remains its ground and the source of its examples. But its principles are set at the level of meaning, and hold wherever meaning must reach an eye: a Markdown file, an HTML page, a status screen. Obsidian, a code editor, a browser, a bare terminal — implementations change, principles hold. Aisthesis does not claim graphic design in general; it claims readability wherever someone works with text and its forms.

What depends on a tool or a medium is kept apart, so that the core travels without it:

- [colour-and-type](references/colour-and-type.md) — colour, typeface, signs, state signals: choosing the means once the meaning is decided
- [convention-callouts](references/convention-callouts.md) — which callouts to use, and how many
- [callouts-reference](references/callouts-reference.md) — available callout types
- [mermaid-reference](references/mermaid-reference.md) — supported diagram types

The developed text of what this head states — [reader](references/reader.md), [whole](references/whole.md), the latter with the characteristics of a readable workspace and what remains to explore — is read on demand.

---

## Relation to Genesis and Praxis

Genesis designs the workspace — *what* goes *where*. Praxis operates it — *what to do*. Aisthesis makes it readable — *how* meaning becomes visible, and *what interaction* a document expects. The three form the Topos triptych: design, operate, perceive. Aisthesis prescribes neither structure nor action: form in service of meaning.
