# OBSERVE

Draw from an experience a view on one aspect.

**When:** Something happened — in the forge, in the world, in conversation — and it says something about an aspect worth thinking through.

**Input:** Lived experience or documented input in the `0.` folders.

**Output:** An adjustment — an existing fragment enriched, a new fragment, or a lateral directive revised.

**Sequence:**
1. What happened?
2. What does it say — about which aspect?
3. Where does it belong? An existing fragment it enriches, a new fragment, or a lateral — it may turn out to be a constraint or a choice rather than a view.
4. Write the view there, with its conditions — not the case. The case stays in level 0.

**Guardrails:** Observing is the gesture; what is written is the view the observation reveals. A fragment carries a view on one aspect — high-level, with its conditions of applicability, without date or told case. What depends on the moment or the place goes elsewhere: a *how* to Tactics or Operational, a dimension with two open poles to Angles, a decision for our situation to Position. An observation triggers a reflection; it is not filed on arrival — asking where it belongs before writing is what keeps the corpus from swelling one file per event. Fragments from unrecorded experience are valid. Do not consolidate while observing — name first, connect later.

**Source method:** Kyklos (common trunk).
