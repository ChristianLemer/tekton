# DOCUMENT

Produce the orchestration of a unit: what is there, what matters, in what order — and what moved. And the home page of the destination that holds the units.

## Intention

No piece carries the big picture; each speaks about itself. Orchestration makes the composition explicit for the consumer of a unit — and carries the movements, so that a consumer without a carrier still learns where what they knew has gone. One level up, the destination needs the same: someone arriving there must learn what it offers, which unit to start with, and how to install — and that page, too, must have a source.

## When

- A unit is assembled for the first time
- The composition of a unit changed — a piece added, removed, reassigned, split
- The instance records movements that no unit has carried yet
- A new audience must be addressed for an existing unit
- A unit is added to or removed from a destination — its home page no longer says what it offers

## Input

- The unit in the apparatus, with its inventory
- The instance — composition, reading order, movements
- The system file — where the system has a place for orchestration, and in what form
- Knowledge of the audience(s) — human reader, agent, both
- For the home page: every unit the destination offers, with its presentation, and the destination's channels from the instance

## Output

One orchestration artifact per unit, and one home page per destination, written into the apparatus, in the place and form the system file gives. The orchestration carries the inventory of what the unit carries, what is constitutive and what is referential for each audience, the order in which the unit's pieces are read and why, and the movements since the previous version.

## Sequence

1. **Inventory** what the unit carries, from PACKAGE's inventory — never from the deliverables directly, so that what is described is what is shipped.
2. **Classify** constitutive versus referential, per audience. The distinction is a property of the relationship between a piece and its audience, not of the piece.
3. **State the order** in which the unit's pieces are read — the corpus's reading order, restricted to the unit's selection, as the inventory renders it — and why: what each piece presupposes or frames, read where REVIEW reads it, in what the deliverables say of their neighbours. The instance keeps the order, not its reasons.
4. **Carry the movements.** From the instance: what left which piece for where, what is retired. Written for someone who knew the previous version and opens this one.
5. **Write the technical content** — inventory, order, dependencies, how to start.
6. **Write the promotional content** where the unit is meant to be discovered — honest articulation of why one would care, before how it works.
7. **Write the destination's home page** — what it offers, which unit to start with and why, how to install from each channel — from the units' presentations and the instance, never from memory of the last page.

## Guardrails

- **One orchestration per unit, one home page per destination, not one per audience.** Sections when needs diverge, never two documents for the same target. A different system has its own orchestration — the forms do not substitute.
- **It describes, it does not know.** The orchestration carries the coherence of the whole; it has no knowledge of its own. A sentence here that no deliverable says is substance born at the target.
- **Regenerated, never maintained.** It is written from the inventory and the instance; editing it by hand makes it the one thing in the unit with no source.
- **Promotional content is honest value, not marketing.**
