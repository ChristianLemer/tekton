# ADMIT

Orient a new piece at the moment its subject is born.

## Intention

Tell the author where the piece goes in the corpus and what it will have to carry to leave it one day — before the workshop opens, while it still costs nothing. A piece that learns these things at exit learns them late, and the whole learns of the piece later still.

## When

- A new subject appears and someone is about to open a workshop for it
- An existing piece is about to split, merge, or change nature
- A piece that was never admitted comes up for the first time at a review

## Input

- The subject, as the author states it
- Where the corpus explains itself — its places, the criterion each one declares in its own README, and the order in which they are asked where they may overlap
- The instance (`MANIFEST.md`, at the head of the corpus's place) — the units, their composition rules or lists
- Agora's consigne to pieces — what every piece declares (see QUALIFY, *what a piece declares*)
- The system file's *threshold*, for each system the piece will be worked in — the executable form of a declared method

## Output

- The piece inscribed in the instance: added to the list of the unit it will enter, where that unit's composition is a list; a rule catches it by itself
- The author knows: the place and its criterion, the nature the piece will have, whether a composition rule already catches it, the three declarations its README must carry, and the executable form of its source method
- **No workshop is created and nothing is written in the piece.** The author does that.

## Sequence

1. **Place it.** Which place's criterion does the subject meet? Ask the criteria in the order the corpus declares; say the criterion, not just the place — a placement that cannot name its criterion is a resemblance, not a decision. One piece, one place. If no criterion fits, the piece is not misfiled: a place is missing. Propose one, with its criterion as a question a piece answers yes or no, and let the one who assembles the corpus confirm it; that place's README then carries the criterion — written by whoever assembles the corpus, not by this gesture.
2. **Name its nature.** Grammar, method, persona — or whatever this corpus has named in addition. The nature decides how the piece will render in each system; it is the author's to declare, this gesture only asks.
3. **Check the rules.** Does a unit's composition rule already designate this piece once it exists? Say so: the author should know that delivering means going out — to a few first. If the rule would make the unit exceed a limit of its system, say that too — the instance carries the composition, the system file carries the limit.
4. **Hand over the consigne.** The three declarations the README must carry — nature, source method, conditions of application — and the presentation (`ABOUT.md`: name, what it is about, when it serves — the last as the situations that call the piece, since that is what a system reads to decide) the deliverable must carry. And, for the source method, its executable form in the system in use, as that system's file names it under *threshold*: the README says *which* method to whoever reads, the executable form says *what to do* to whoever acts in the piece, and both are committed with it. For a persona, or a method held by a guardian, the marks the system file sets — where packaging cuts and what it derives the address from: they are what lets the piece render without the tooling knowing its name. This is Agora's only instruction to a cycle: what it must *carry*, never how it must *work*.
5. **Inscribe.** Where the unit's composition is a list, add the piece to it; where it is a rule, the rule already holds it. Nothing else changes in the instance: until the piece has an accepted deliverable, the trace says it is still being designed, and nothing goes out.

## Guardrails

- **Guide, do not forge.** ADMIT says where and what; the workshop is the author's. Writing the first fragment "to help" is a piece edited from outside its cycle.
- **A criterion, not a resemblance.** « It looks like the others in this place » is how a ranking stops being arbitrable. Name the declared criterion or report that none fits.
- **Nothing is decided about exit.** Being inscribed is not a promise to ship. How far a version goes, and whether the whole holds the piece back, is decided at REVIEW, once the author has delivered.
- **Skipping ADMIT is not fatal.** REVIEW finds an unadmitted piece at the next exit and asks the same questions — later, with less to gain. Say that to an author who hesitates; do not make admission a toll.
