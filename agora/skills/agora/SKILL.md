# Agora

*The guardian of the whole.*

A method for keeping a living corpus coherent from the moment a piece is born to the moment it reaches a consumer — what enters, what is configured to leave, what leaves.

---

## The Problem

A single artifact is self-contained. But when artifacts articulate — when one completes another, when one only works in the light of another — they form a **corpus**: a whole whose coherence is a property to manage.

Inside the source workspace, the links between artifacts are implicit. The producer knows the context. The moment those artifacts leave their workshop and reach consumers who do not share the production context, coherence is no longer free. Each consumer must reconstruct the topology alone — an experienced human manages by intuition, an AI agent does not manage at all.

Agora exists because the corpus as a whole has properties that no other method manages. Kyklos forges artifacts. Genesis structures workspaces. Praxis operates within them. But when artifacts articulate into a whole, leave their workshop, and must make themselves understood — that is Agora's territory.

---

## Part I — Grammar

### Corpus

A corpus is born when artifacts can no longer be understood in isolation. The criterion is not quantity — it is **mutual dependency of meaning**. Two interdependent artifacts already form a corpus.

What distinguishes a corpus from a collection: **coherence** (shared vocabulary, conventions, cross-references — managed, not accidental), **topology** (dependencies structure the whole; a reading order exists), and **completeness** (a gap affects the whole, not just the missing piece).

The purpose of a corpus is not to gather artifacts — it is to deliver a **coherent product** to the consumer.

### Grammar and Method

Each piece of a corpus carries two layers of knowledge:

- **Grammar** — the concepts, the vocabulary, the fundamental distinctions. What one must understand to think in the domain.
- **Method** — the gestures, the sequences, the recipes. What one must do to act in the domain.

Grammar makes an operator **autonomous** — able to judge whether a situation is covered by the method or not. Method makes an operator **effective** — able to act when the situation is clear.

A corpus that publishes only its gestures without its concepts condemns its consumers to mechanical execution. A corpus that publishes only its concepts without its gestures condemns its consumers to enlightened inaction.

These are the two layers of *knowledge*. What a corpus carries besides — what mobilises it, when it applies, how it is arranged — is the matter of the sections that follow.

### Nature and Form

A piece **declares its nature** — grammar, method, persona, or whatever a corpus has named in addition. The nature says what the piece *is*; it is the author's to declare and it does not change with the destination.

What the piece *becomes* in a given system — a skill, an agent file, a page — is decided by the system, from the nature. The same grammar renders differently in two systems; the same system renders a grammar and a persona differently. Nature is a fact of the piece; form is a fact of the system. Agora keeps the two apart: the piece never carries the vocabulary of a system it will be rendered into, and the knowledge of each system is a detachable file in Agora's own deliverable.

### Stratification

The pieces of a corpus are not laid side by side — they build on each other.

Grammars **stratify**: each artifact extends the vocabulary laid down by those that precede it. Methods **compose**: one uses another, applies it, builds on its gestures.

The corpus is therefore not a mosaic but a stack. Reading order is dictated by presuppositions, not by choice. Completeness is fragile downward — removing a lower stratum destabilises everything above. Bootstrap has a direction.

Packaging itself obeys this. The knowledge of how a corpus is wrapped for a system is not a parallel grammar laid beside the corpus: it is a higher stratum that presupposes it. And the knowledge of one particular system is a stratum higher still — thin, graftable, removable without touching the core.

### A Method Needs Its Conditions of Application

A piece is not only what it knows. It is also what it needs to act: a carrier that loads it, a trigger that calls it, or nothing — it applies by default, without being asked. Two methods of equal maturity may differ only there, and that difference decides where each one can go.

Hence a piece in the corpus is **not thereby deployed** — and leaving is not all or nothing. A piece goes more or less far: still being designed, explored with a few, published to all. Or it is paused, for a reason of the whole's own. None of these is a defect. Being in the corpus is a fact of the whole; how far a piece goes is its author's to say, and the author says it first by acting — delivering, publishing is itself information about where the piece stands.

### Leaving the Workshop

Publishing an artifact is not copying it from one place to another. The crossing changes the nature of the demand on the artifact.

In its workshop, a deliverable is surrounded by context — the intention, the fragments, the compromises that shaped it. When the artifact leaves, all of that stays behind. The consumer receives the bare product.

Leaving the workshop imposes **self-sufficiency**: what is published must carry enough meaning to be understood without its birth ecosystem. Publication is an act of **decontextualisation** — conscious, not out of bureaucracy, but out of necessity.

Self-sufficiency is not verified artifact by artifact — it is guaranteed by **homologating the production method**. A project that follows Kyklos is homologated by construction. Any other process must demonstrate that it guarantees self-sufficiency. Homologation bears on the process, not the product.

### Every Deliverable Carries Its Presentation

An artifact leaving its workshop must be able to say what it is and when it serves. This is self-sufficiency taken one level down: no longer the space that explains itself, but each piece.

The two are **two texts, for two readers**. *When it serves* is the back cover: read before opening, by whoever decides whether to open. *What it is about* is read once opened. A system may read only the first — fused into one text, it could no longer derive the one it reads.

That presentation is **a deliverable in its own right** — forged, reviewed, verifiable, like the content. It is not *generated* from the content (extracting the first sentence does not produce a reliable presentation), and it is not *optional* — a deliverable without one is incomplete.

What this buys: packaging **translates, it does not write**. Whatever metadata a system demands derives from a presentation the artifact already carries. Nothing is authored outside the workshop.

### What Only the Whole Can See

Some decisions do not exist at the level of a piece. Not through incompetence — through **lack of information**. A piece cannot know it is the only one doing otherwise, nor what its name would collide with, nor whether the unit it enters has room for it.

| What escapes the piece | Why |
|---|---|
| the big picture | each artifact speaks about itself |
| packaging — scope, non-collision, triggers, limits | depends on what exists elsewhere |
| conformity to conventions | reads in the gap to siblings, not in itself |
| whether to hold a piece back | a decision about what the corpus presents, not about the piece |

The cost of getting this wrong is asymmetric. Deciding globally what was local produces bureaucracy. Deciding locally what was global produces **silent drift**: every piece coherent with itself, the whole no longer coherent, and nobody sees it.

### Omission Is Structural

Nothing in an artifact signals what it does not say — which is why detection cannot come from it.

A stale artifact looks exactly like a current one, and keeps presenting itself as current. A *missing* document is immediately visible; a *frozen* one is not. The same holds for packaging: wrapping resembles copying, and with a single format it is — the transformation only appears when the target form has its own grammar. And the same holds for the whole's own state: a unit that carries four pieces where five were declared looks exactly like a complete unit.

Hence the detection mechanism: not confrontation with a rule — the special case can always be pleaded — but **lateral contrast**. What do the siblings do? A written convention invites debate; unanimous practice minus one exception does not. **It is not rules that set the norm, it is neighbours.**

And hence the second mechanism: **declare, then compare.** What the whole holds is written down, and every gesture reads it; what is written is compared to what is there, at every use. A declaration nobody rereads drifts without anyone erring.

### The Living Corpus

A corpus is not a finished product. It has an **intention** — a reason why these artifacts belong together and not others. Every addition is a decision confronted with it. It has **conscious gaps** — pieces missing and known to be missing; an identified gap is a plan, an ignored one a debt. And it has a **trajectory**.

A corpus does not evolve only piece by piece. It grows, shrinks, splits, merges; a section leaves one grammar for another; a method is retired. Each of these movements affects coherence, topology, and what consumers thought they knew. The whole must be able to say what moved.

### Kept, Arranged, Housed

A corpus exists as a fact, as soon as artifacts depend on one another. **Keeping it is decided.** Nothing obliges it — a corpus nobody keeps is not at fault. What is decided is declared, the way one declares a method followed, and at the same two addresses: a name for whoever comes to understand, an instruction for whoever comes to act.

A kept corpus is **arranged**, and an arrangement can be discussed only if each place says what it takes in; without a declared criterion, where a piece goes is settled by habit, and a new piece has nothing to be measured against. From a partition, the arrangement keeps two properties — one place per piece, one criterion per place — and lets go of the third: a living corpus cannot hold completeness in advance. A piece that finds no place is not misfiled; it signals a place that is missing — a conscious gap. Criteria of one order make a partition, where a piece's place can be read. Criteria of different orders overlap, and then a declared priority decides — otherwise usage does, silently. The arrangement aims at coherence, not purity.

And a corpus needs **a place of its own**. It holds things that belong to no piece: what the whole knows of itself, what it assembles to leave, what the destination receives without its coming from a piece. They live beside the pieces. As long as the corpus is all its workspace holds, the corpus's place and the workspace coincide, and the confusion cannot be seen; once it shares the space, what belongs to the whole stays at the workspace's root, far from the pieces, and the method that keeps the corpus names places it does not own. So Agora describes **the inside of the folder it is given**, and never names what contains it — the same step a method for a single piece takes when it stops naming its container.

### Cadence

AI accelerates the forge. The consequence is not just higher volume — it is structural pressure on everything that comes after. What worked by human memory now requires a mechanism — and the mechanism threatens the judgment it serves. The tension is not resolved in general: it is decided gesture by gesture, by whether judgment disappears or not.

The right balance: the automaton executes, the operator decides when to trigger.

### Verifiability

A published corpus must be verifiable at any moment: does what is in service match the source, and does the source match what the whole declares?

The approach is **declarative** — compare current states, no need to track past acts. Verification does not count what is present; it asks what each thing determines, and stops where nothing is written. Its boundary is *declared or undeclared*, not *configuration or quality*: it can compare a form to a written form, never judge whether the written form is right.

### Making Itself Understood

A corpus that is merely *available* will only be adopted by those who already know it exists. Making itself understood requires making itself **desirable** — why should I care? — and **usable** — how do I get started? Both are products of corpus management, not optional extras.

Three ends do not substitute for one another: to understand, to execute, to discover. A corpus that serves only one is not incomplete in content — it is closed to the other ways in. Serving one at a time is a transitional state, assumed as a conscious gap — never a doctrine.

## Part II — Method

### The Guardian and Its Three Moments

Agora is the **guardian of the whole**, and it holds the three moments of that role: a piece **enters** the corpus; the whole is **configured** — what leaves, in what, for which system; something **leaves**. One method, one name. The one who recommends the structure of the corpus is the one who delivers it — a method that took notes at entry for another to consolidate at exit would lose information at every handover.

Towards a piece, Agora does three things and never a fourth. **Upstream, it guides**: it says what a piece must carry to leave — nature, the method it follows, presentation, conditions of application — as an instruction addressed to the piece's cycle. **Meanwhile, it lets be**: the cycle forges, decides, distils. **Downstream, it verifies**: it rereads whether the instruction was kept, and reports the gap. **It never modifies a piece.** Modification belongs to the cycle that holds the piece. The seam between Agora and that cycle is a reading in each direction — the instruction one way, the deliverable the other — with no memory between them.

### Three Tiers, One Place Per Fact

What is known at one tier is written at that tier, once. Agora crosses the tiers; it does not copy them.

| Tier | What it is | What it writes | Who writes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the method** | Agora's deliverable — this one, generic | the form of an instance, the gestures, the instruction to pieces, and one file per target system | Agora |
| **the instance** | Agora applied to one corpus — `MANIFEST.md`, at the head of the corpus's place | the units that leave, each one's composition by rule or by list, the target system and its channels, the pauses, the record of movements, the review trace | whoever assembles the corpus |
| **the piece** | the cycle of one artifact | its nature, the method it follows, its conditions of application, its presentation, the inventory of its deliverable | the piece's author, through its cycle |

The parallel is Kyklos: one method, workshops that are its instances, and inside each workshop a content that belongs to it alone. Agora repeats the pattern one tier up.

The split forbids two things. The instance does not restate what a piece declares of itself — its nature is read at the piece. And the piece does not say what the whole decides — a **pause**, holding a piece back, is written in the instance, with its reason.

**Maturity has no tier: it has a gesture.** No tier writes it. It is read in the trace of what was done for the piece — a deliverable accepted or not, a version served to a few or to all — and confirmed aloud when something leaves. A trace cannot go stale: it *is* the act. Only the pause is written, because no act carries it: a piece held back looks exactly like a piece forgotten. Agora crosses three things — the rule that designates a piece, the trace of what was done for it, the pause that holds it — and where they disagree, it shows the gap to the user, never resolves it alone.

### The Place of the Corpus

Agora prescribes what is inside the folder it is given, and nothing outside it:

```
<the folder you are given>/
├── MANIFEST.md     ← the instance: what leaves, in which units, for where
├── <system>.json   ← what the corpus has read for a system — one per system file its units target
├── 0. Colophon/    ← what the destination receives that derives from no piece
├── 1. Corpus/      ← the pieces, arranged in places
└── 2. Apparatus/   ← the product of the corpus: one folder per unit
```

**The pieces are arranged in places**, and each place states its criterion in its own README, as a question a piece answers yes or no. Where the corpus explains itself, it lists its places with their criteria — and, where they may overlap, the order in which they are asked. A piece lives in one place.

**The instance sits beside the pieces, not among them**: it is not a piece, it says what is done with the pieces. **The apparatus is the corpus's product** — to the corpus what a piece's product is to the piece; it lives inside, not beside. Numbering follows Genesis inside a zone: a sequence — the pieces, then what is assembled from them — and zero for what enters outside the sequence. `1. Corpus/` and `2. Apparatus/` are the template's names; a corpus may declare others in its instance, and everything that reads the corpus takes them from there. `MANIFEST.md`, `0. Colophon/` and the readings files keep their names: the gestures find them by these. **What a corpus reads for a system is the corpus's**, not the method's — the descriptions compressed from each presentation, what no piece carries but the system requires: it sits beside the instance, never beside the tooling, so that the tooling serves any corpus as it is.

**A piece's product is read where the method it follows puts it** — for a piece that follows Kyklos, `4. Forge/<PRODUCT>/`, of which the accepted state is the deliverable. A piece whose product cannot be found there stops PACKAGE, which names it.

**Gestures and tools start from this folder** and find everything relative to it. Where the folder sits in the workspace, and how the workspace says that it hands this folder to Agora, belongs to the workspace's structure — Genesis. Agora only asks that it be said.

### The Instance

A corpus kept by Agora has one file at the head of its place, `MANIFEST.md`, in the form Agora prescribes (`templates/instance.md`). It is named for what it is — the manifest of what leaves — and not for the method that reads it. Six sections, each stating whether it can go stale:

| Section | Says | Regime |
|---|---|---|
| **Units** | the folders of the pieces and of the apparatus; each installable whole: name, system, destination; and per destination, its channels — catalogue name, served to a few or to all | declared |
| **Reading order** | the order in which pieces are read together, once for the whole corpus — the logic of their combination. A unit's order is this one restricted to its selection, derived and written nowhere | decided — read in the topology, revalidated when a piece enters it |
| **Composition** | per unit, a rule *or* a list, and what the unit adds — at most one unit per destination adds a persona that holds the main thread | rule derived; list and additions decided |
| **Pauses** | pieces the rules designate and the whole holds back, and why. A piece with no accepted deliverable is not listed: the trace says it already | decided — revalidated at each review |
| **Movements** | what left which piece for where, what is retired | decided — written when confirmed, erased once delivered |
| **Last review** | the corpus revision at the last REVIEW | what PACKAGE requires |

How pieces combine is a judgment, and it belongs to the gesture that decides what leaves: REVIEW reads what the deliverables say of their neighbours, tells what a piece *presupposes* from what it *frames* — a lens it serves without presupposing it —, proposes the order, flags a selection that takes a piece without what it presupposes, and the user confirms. No piece has to declare its dependencies in an imposed form; whoever composes a unit asks for that judgment, or reads what the instance decided.

Composition is said **by rule or by list**, at the corpus's choice. Both fail differently; the review at each use catches the failure of either. The instance is in the artifact-layer language — gestures read it.

### The Apparatus

A unit belongs to no piece: it assembles pieces from many workshops. It is therefore assembled outside every workshop, in the **apparatus** of the corpus — a folder of the corpus's place, beside the pieces and the instance, one subfolder per unit. Versioned with the corpus, because the diff between two packagings is worth reading. It is what DEPLOY compares to the destination and what DOCUMENT reads. Nothing is ever assembled directly at a destination: what is written at the target has no source.

### What the Destination Holds

A destination is a shelf: it decides nothing, and nothing is written there by hand. The apparatus is therefore the **complete image of what the destination holds**, and each thing there has its source and its gesture:

| At the destination | Comes from | Gesture |
|---|---|---|
| the units | the pieces' deliverables, per the instance's composition | PACKAGE |
| each channel's catalogue — the units it offers | the instance's units, and each unit's manifest | PACKAGE generates it |
| the home page — what the destination offers, how to install | the units' presentations, and the instance | DOCUMENT |
| what is served to all rather than to a few | the user's answer at review | REVIEW decides, DEPLOY carries |
| files that derive from no piece — the licence | a file in `0. Colophon/`, which the instance names | PACKAGE copies it |
| the rules of an agent started at the destination — commit and push, nothing else | an `AGENTS.md` in `0. Colophon/`, written from the template, named as a destination file — filed there, it never instructs an agent working in the corpus | PACKAGE copies it |

**One more unit is one more line in the units** — decided at review, with the user; the catalogue follows. **A channel is written once**, in the instance: its catalogue name, and whom it serves. Neither the number nor the name of a unit repeats it. Whatever sits at the destination without coming from the apparatus is a gap — a remnant of a time it was worked in, or a text retouched there: DEPLOY shows it first, dry; the applied alignment removes it. What version control ignores there is never touched. **The destination is the only output:** what is distributed leaves from it, and from nowhere else.

### Three Criteria, Three Natures

What leaves is decided by crossing three things that are not of the same kind:

| Criterion | Question | Nature | Lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **belonging** | does the piece fall under the unit? | derived by rule, without memory | the composition, in the instance |
| **maturity** | how far does this version go? | an author's appreciation, read in their gesture and **confirmed** aloud | the trace: deliverable accepted or not, version served to a few or to all |
| **pause** | do we hold it back? | a decision of the whole — it **decides** | the pauses, in the instance |

Silence counts as candidacy: a piece the rule designates comes up even if it declares nothing, and the review asks. What is automated is the question, never the answer — a piece delivered since the last review does not enter a unit by itself. Push is conscious.

**To a few first, to all when the author says so.** A new version, the review proposes to send to a few; a version already served to a few and unchanged since, it asks whether it goes to all. The author follows or corrects — a minimal change may go straight to all, a simple-looking one may stay on trial. What is confirmed is where *this version* goes, not a rank attached to the piece; the answer is written nowhere — the transport that follows leaves its trace.

### Systems

Agora's deliverable carries one file per target system in `SYSTEMS/`. Each answers the same questions in the same order — the unit, the rendering of each nature, the metadata keys and what they derive from, the declared transformations, the limits, where the number lives, the gate, the comparison — so that PACKAGE and DEPLOY know where to read. A system file contains only facts of the system, verified on real artifacts; its tooling lives beside it. It is removable when a system is no longer targeted and graftable for a new one, without touching the method.

### Gestures

Six gestures, on three moments. See [GESTURES/](GESTURES/).

| Moment | Gesture | Does |
|---|---|---|
| **entry** | [ADMIT](GESTURES/ADMIT.md) | orient a new piece: where it goes and by which criterion — and, when no place fits, propose a new one with its criterion —, its nature, what it will have to carry, whether a rule already catches it. Inscribes it in the instance. Guides; does not forge |
| **configuration** | [REVIEW](GESTURES/REVIEW.md) | pass the instance in review with the user: derive, read, detect, show, ask, write; check that each piece has one place and each place its criterion — and propose how far each new version goes. Systematic, even without a clue. Leaves the trace every exit requires |
| **exit** | [QUALIFY](GESTURES/QUALIFY.md) | report whether the pieces are fit to leave — against what they declare and the corpus's conventions. Reports; never repairs |
| | [PACKAGE](GESTURES/PACKAGE.md) | assemble, in the apparatus, the units and everything the destination holds. Refuses what is not conform, produces only what the review confirmed, blocks a changed content under an unchanged number |
| | [DEPLOY](GESTURES/DEPLOY.md) | carry the apparatus to the channel the review chose: a clean destination, no served number under another content, then align. Mechanical, idempotent, dry-run by default |
| | [DOCUMENT](GESTURES/DOCUMENT.md) | produce the orchestration of a unit — what is there, what matters, in what order, what moved — and the destination's home page |

Only ADMIT and REVIEW write in the instance. No gesture writes in a piece. Every exit begins with REVIEW: PACKAGE refuses without a review of the current revision.

Two separations hold the exit gestures: **packaging is not transport** — one produces an inspectable unit, the other carries it; **verification informs, packaging commits** — the first may be mechanical, the second must be able to refuse.

### The Passage

1. **REVIEW** — derive the candidates by rule, read each at home and in its trace, show what changed, propose how far each new version goes and reread the pauses, piece by piece, write the instance.
2. **QUALIFY** — reread each piece that leaves against what it declares and the conventions. Report.
3. **PACKAGE** — assemble in the apparatus, and **stop at the first missing input**.
4. **Compare** with what is in service, from the apparatus.
5. **DEPLOY** — carry, to the channel the review chose. **DOCUMENT** — orchestrate.

A stop is a product, not a failure. Nobody surveys in advance what is missing: the gesture names it, by hitting it. If the missing input is of a kind another corpus would have to provide too, it enters the form of the instance; if no corpus could provide it, the defect is Agora's.

### Movements

A change between pieces is **applied** — no old form preserved alongside — but the corpus **knows what it did**. REVIEW records the movement when the user confirms it; PACKAGE embarks it in the unit; the carrier tells the consumer **once**, at the first session after the update; DOCUMENT keeps it in the orchestration for those without a carrier. The producer pushes, but it pushes while warning.

### Tooling, and Its Limits

Tooling covers what is mechanical: comparing states, transporting, recording that a presentation or a declaration is present, detecting that a piece appeared or a deliverable changed, generating what is integrally derivable. Dry-run by default for anything that touches the outside.

What tooling cannot do: judge the quality of a presentation, write metadata for a system, verify a non-collision, decide a pause, how far a version goes, or a version level. Each requires knowing the whole or writing prose. The switch to tooling happens *when judgment disappears* — and for packaging it does not. **More tooling is not more discipline.**

## Applying Agora

### For whoever assembles a corpus

1. **Decide to keep the corpus, and declare it** — where the workspace names the methods it follows, and where its agents read their instructions; say where the corpus's place is.
2. In that place, copy `templates/instance.md` as `MANIFEST.md`; fill in the folders, the units and their composition. In `0. Colophon/`, write the destination's `AGENTS.md` from `templates/destination-agents.md`, and whatever else the destination receives that derives from no piece.
3. Arrange the pieces in places, each stating its criterion; give the corpus an **apparatus** — the folder where units assemble.
4. When a subject is born, **ADMIT** it.
5. Before anything leaves, **REVIEW** — and let it ask.
6. **QUALIFY**, **PACKAGE**, **DEPLOY**, **DOCUMENT**, in that order, and stop where a gesture stops.
7. What a gesture finds wrong in a piece goes back to that piece's cycle. Never fix it from here.

### For the author of a piece

Your README answers three questions — what is it, which method does it follow, what does it need to act — and your deliverable carries its presentation. How far your piece goes, you say by acting: delivering it is the first word, and when it leaves, Agora tells you what it reads and asks where this version goes. That is all Agora asks of you. Everything else is yours.

### For a consumer

Start at the orchestration of the unit you received — it tells you what is here, what matters, in what order, and what moved since the version you knew.

## Relationship to Other Methods

| Method | Boundary with Agora |
|---|---|
| **Kyklos** | Forges and delivers one piece, in its workshop. Agora reads the deliverable — where Kyklos puts the product — and the README; it writes neither. The seam is a reading in each direction, with no memory between. |
| **Genesis** | Designs the workspace: where the corpus's place sits, and how the workspace says it hands that place to Agora. And the README, where a piece declares what Agora reads. |
| **Praxis** | Gesture anatomy; the test *does judgment disappear?* that splits tooling from gesture. |
| **Prosopon** | Names the personas and what a carrier mobilises. Agora reads a persona's nature; Prosopon says what a persona is. |
| **Kentauros** | Any act crossing a boundary is conscious. Foundational for Agora. |

## Dependencies

- **Genesis** — workspace conventions, where the corpus's place sits and how it is declared, the README as the piece's declaration
- **Praxis** — gesture anatomy, tooling boundary
- **Kentauros** — conscious boundary crossing
